## 1. What is complementary feeding?

Complementary feeding is the process of introducing foods in addition to milk when breast milk or infant formula alone is no longer sufficient to meet a child's nutritional requirements.

According to WHO, complementary feeding generally begins at around 6 months of age and continues until 23 months.

This period is important for:
- Developing acceptance of healthy foods and beverages.
- Establishing long-term dietary patterns.
- Supporting nutritional needs during early childhood.
- Addressing the increased risk of growth faltering and nutrient deficiencies.

## 2. When should complementary feeding begin?

WHO recommends introducing complementary foods at 6 months (180 days), while continuing breastfeeding.

**Recommendation strength:** Strong recommendation.

**Certainty of evidence:** Low certainty.

This is a public health recommendation. Individual concerns about feeding adequacy should be discussed with an appropriate healthcare professional.

## 3. Dietary diversity

WHO recommends that infants and young children aged 6–23 months consume a diverse diet.

The guideline includes the following recommendations:

### 3.1 Animal-source foods

Animal-source foods, including meat, fish and eggs, should be consumed daily.

- Recommendation strength: Strong.
- Certainty of evidence: Low.

### 3.2 Fruits and vegetables

Fruits and vegetables should be consumed daily.

- Recommendation strength: Strong.
- Certainty of evidence: Low.

### 3.3 Pulses, nuts and seeds

Pulses, nuts and seeds should be consumed frequently, particularly when meat, fish, eggs and vegetables are limited in the diet.

- Recommendation strength: Conditional.
- Certainty of evidence: Very low.

These recommendations are specific to children aged 6–23 months. Food preparation, texture and choking safety require age-appropriate consideration.

## 4. Responsive feeding

WHO recommends responsive feeding for children aged 6–23 months.

Responsive feeding means recognizing and responding to a child's physiological and developmental needs during feeding.

It encourages children to eat in response to their hunger and fullness signals and supports their developing autonomy.

- Recommendation strength: Strong.
- Certainty of evidence: Low.

## 5. Important considerations

- Complementary foods are introduced in addition to milk; they do not automatically replace breastfeeding.
- Recommendations in this document apply to children aged 6–23 months.
- Do not assume that every recommendation applies identically to every child.
- Individual medical and nutritional concerns require appropriate professional assessment.
- Food safety and age-appropriate preparation must be considered when discussing foods for young children.
