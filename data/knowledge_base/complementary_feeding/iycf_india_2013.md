## 1. What is IYCF?

Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) refers to recommended feeding and care practices for newborns and children under two years of age.

The Ministry's guide identifies four key practices:

1. Early initiation of breastfeeding.
2. Exclusive breastfeeding during the first six months.
3. Introduction of appropriate complementary foods from six months.
4. Continued breastfeeding for two years or beyond.

## 2. Early initiation of breastfeeding

The guideline recommends initiating breastfeeding as early as possible after birth, preferably within the first hour.

The guideline also emphasizes that colostrum should be fed to the newborn rather than discarded.

## 3. Exclusive breastfeeding

The guideline describes exclusive breastfeeding during the first six months as providing breast milk without other foods or liquids, including water.

It identifies specific exceptions, including ORS, oral vaccines, and medically required vitamin, mineral or medicine preparations.

## 4. Complementary feeding

Complementary feeding means introducing suitable foods alongside continued breastfeeding after the child reaches six months of age.

The guide describes complementary foods as needing to be:

- Timely: Introduced when breast milk alone is no longer sufficient to meet nutritional needs.
- Adequate: Providing sufficient energy, protein and micronutrients.
- Safe: Prepared and stored hygienically.

## 5. Age-appropriate feeding

The guide identifies the following practices for children aged 6–23 months:

- Continue breastfeeding alongside complementary feeding.
- Offer foods from a diverse range of food groups.
- Consider age-appropriate feeding frequency.
- Encourage active and responsive feeding.

The guide's operational section discusses food-group diversity and minimum feeding frequency. Exact frequency recommendations must be interpreted according to the child's age and breastfeeding status.

## 6. Community-level support

The guideline emphasizes that IYCF counselling and support should be provided through health facilities, community outreach and home-based care.

It identifies frontline health workers, including ASHAs and ANMs, as important participants in reinforcing appropriate feeding practices.

This is relevant to AANCHAL's Anganwadi-focused awareness use case.
